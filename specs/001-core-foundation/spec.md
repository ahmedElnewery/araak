# Feature Specification: Core Foundation

**Feature Branch:** 001-core-foundation  
**Created:** 2026-10-15  
**Status:** Draft — pending project-owner specification approval  
**Input:** Ratified Project Constitution v1.0.0 (2026-10-15), incorporating 38 project-owner decisions.

## Overview

Deliver the shared foundation for a white-label, multi-tenant charitable-organization management portal before any business-domain module is built. The foundation serves two distinct audiences: a platform Super Admin using a separate administration application, and staff/tenant managers operating inside their own organization's subdomain. It covers organization lifecycle, tenant-scoped identities and invitations, secure sessions, staff management, customizable multiple-role authorization, scoped permissions, audit history, tenant identity, Arabic/English accessibility, theme preferences, modular navigation, and safe production readiness.

**Governing rule:** The ratified constitution at .specify/memory/constitution.md takes precedence. This is a WHAT/WHY specification; detailed architecture, schemas, API contracts, data security mechanisms, UI designs, and deployment procedures belong to subsequent planning artifacts.

### In scope

- Separate platform-administration and tenant-staff experiences sharing one coherent component/design system.
- Platform-controlled organization creation, activation, suspension, initial tenant-manager invitation, and tenant directory.
- Immutable, unique, organization-specific subdomain as the tenant context; no tenant chooser for staff.
- Staff invitation, activation, sign-in using tenant-local username or email plus password, sign-out, verified-email password recovery, and authorized administrative reset.
- Independent staff accounts per tenant, even when the same human email is reused by another tenant.
- Long-lived, revocable, multi-device sessions and staff account activation/disablement.
- Tenant-scoped staff directory, role assignment, custom roles, multiple roles, delegated constrained role management, and permission checks.
- Foundation-level permission/record-scope semantics for All / Own / Assigned; no speculative business-record schema or assignment workflow.
- Audit trail of approved administrative and mutation events; scoped audit-log viewing.
- Tenant required display name, optional logo and neutral fallback; fixed shared colors with personal light/dark/system preference.
- Arabic/RTL and English/LTR experiences and permission-aware, modular application shell.
- Two separated deployment environments and documented release, backup and recovery readiness.

### Out of scope

- Beneficiary, donor, volunteer, donation, accounts/financial, reporting, and Services business records, screens and workflows; their exact order remains undecided.
- Subscription plans, expiry dates, pricing, renewal, billing, payment processing, and subscription invoices.
- Public registration for organizations or employees; beneficiary/donor/volunteer login accounts.
- Tenant deletion; tenant subdomain change; custom domains; tenant color selection.
- MFA/2FA, SMS recovery, tenant switching with a shared cross-tenant staff identity.
- Business dashboards, fabricated KPI widgets, global search, generic custom-field/workflow/form-builder systems, and promotional provider copy.
- Complete record ownership/assignment models or final export/approval semantics for future business modules.
- Production implementation, data model, technology design, or deployment itself: this artifact only specifies accepted behavior.

## User Scenarios & Testing (mandatory)

### User Story 1 — Platform Super Admin onboards and controls an organization (Priority: P1)

As the platform Super Admin, I need to create a tenant with an organization name, a unique subdomain, and a first tenant manager, then activate or suspend the tenant without exposing its protected records.

**Why this priority:** No other tenant-facing workflow can run before authorized tenant provisioning and trustworthy tenant resolution.

**Independent Test:** Create two organizations with distinct subdomains, send the initial manager invitation, suspend one, and verify no access to its operations while the other remains unaffected.

**Acceptance Scenarios:**

1. **Given** an authorized Super Admin, **when** they create an organization with a valid unused subdomain, name, and first manager email, **then** the platform records the tenant, resolves its independent portal, and initiates an authorized first-manager invitation.
2. **Given** a missing organization name, invalid subdomain, or already-used subdomain, **when** creation is attempted, **then** creation is rejected with a non-sensitive, actionable error.
3. **Given** an existing tenant, **when** its organization display name or logo changes, **then** its original subdomain remains unchanged and cannot be edited by either type of administrator.
4. **Given** a tenant with active staff sessions, **when** Super Admin suspends it, **then** further tenant-facing requests and data operations from those sessions are rejected while its data remains intact.
5. **Given** a suspended tenant, **when** Super Admin reactivates it, **then** appropriately authenticated and authorized staff can resume use under their existing roles.
6. **Given** two tenants, **when** the Super Admin manages one, **then** the action does not reveal the other tenant's private records or accidentally suspend it.
7. **Given** a tenant that already has a manager, **when** a properly authorized platform Super Admin appoints another tenant manager by inviting a new employee or promoting an existing tenant employee, **then** the protected manager authority is granted only to the selected staff identity within that tenant and the grant is audited; no beneficiary/donor/financial access is granted to the Super Admin.
8. **Given** a tenant with two active managers, **when** an authorized platform Super Admin revokes the protected tenant-manager authority from one of them, **then** that manager privilege is removed for the specified tenant and the revocation is audited; neither a tenant manager nor delegated role administrator may perform the same operation, including through a direct API or custom role.
9. **Given** an active tenant with only one activated, active tenant manager, **when** anyone (including a Super Admin) attempts to revoke that manager authority without another activated and active manager already in place, **then** the operation is rejected atomically; a pending manager invitation does not count as a replacement. Once another active manager exists, an authorized Super Admin may proceed.
10. **Given** an enabled employee with tenant-manager authority plus one or more ordinary staff roles, **when** an authorized platform Super Admin revokes only the Manager Role, **then** the account stays enabled under the same staff identity, keeps every previously assigned non-manager role unchanged, and immediately loses all manager-only capabilities; existing staff sessions cannot continue exercising the removed privilege. If that was the only role, the enabled account gains no default business/administrative grants.
11. **Given** an authorized Super Admin successfully revokes a staff member's tenant-manager authority, **when** the revocation is committed, **then** ALL existing sessions for that tenant staff account on all browsers/devices are revoked immediately, including refresh/reuse of old tokens, regardless of their remaining ordinary roles. The account remains enabled and the employee may authenticate again with the same credentials and the unchanged non-manager roles. No concurrent request from a previously established session may exercise privileged or other protected operations after the revocation commits.

### User Story 2 — Invited employee activates an independent tenant account and signs in (Priority: P1)

As an invited staff member, I need to choose my own username and password, change my username securely from my own account settings, sign in from my organization's portal using username or email, and recover access using my verified, unchangeable-after-activation email.

**Why this priority:** Staff access is invitation-only and must not confuse two separate tenant accounts that share the same email.

**Independent Test:** Invite a person at each of two tenants using the same real email; demonstrate two separate username/password credentials and sessions, plus tenant-scoped login and recovery. **Blocked until authentication feasibility gate G-01 is passed.**

**Acceptance Scenarios:**

1. **Given** an authorized tenant manager or permitted Super Admin, **when** they invite a staff member by email and assign permitted initial roles, **then** the recipient receives a tenant-appropriate one-time invitation valid for seven days from issuance and no public registration route is offered.
2. **Given** a valid unexpired invitation for tenant A, **when** the invitee chooses an unused tenant-local username and password and completes verification, **then** a tenant-A account is activated with the approved roles.
3. **Given** an already-used username at tenant A, **when** an invitee chooses it, **then** the account cannot be activated with that username; use of the same username at tenant B remains permissible.
4. **Given** an active tenant-A account, **when** staff sign in on tenant A's subdomain with either their local username or email and correct password, **then** they enter tenant A only.
5. **Given** the same real email is invited to tenants A and B, **when** both accounts are activated, **then** they remain independently authorized, may have different usernames and passwords, and cannot log into the wrong tenant using the other tenant's password.
6. **Given** invalid credentials, an expired/reused invitation, unknown host, or a disabled account, **when** a user tries to authenticate, **then** access is refused without leaking another tenant's identity or revealing whether a private account exists.
7. **Given** a user who forgot the password, **when** they use verified-email recovery, **then** only the selected tenant account may be reset; an authorized administrator may initiate a reset without viewing an existing password or impersonating the user.
8. **Given** an email is already registered for one staff account in tenant A, **when** an administrator tries to invite a second account in tenant A with that email, **then** duplicate onboarding is rejected safely; the same email remains eligible for an independent account in tenant B.
9. **Given** an employee has a valid, active, tenant-scoped authenticated session, **when** they change their own username to an available name in Account Settings, **then** no password re-entry, email OTP, or additional verification step is required; only their tenant-local username changes, the same immutable account identity, roles and records remain intact, the change is audited, and future login accepts the new username.
10. **Given** the requested new username is already assigned to another staff account in the same tenant, **when** a change is attempted, **then** it is rejected without disclosing unnecessary account details; a matching username in another tenant does not block the change.
11. **Given** a tenant-bound invitation, **when** the invited person activates within seven days (168 hours) of issuance, **then** the invitation can be redeemed once if all security checks pass; **when** seven days have elapsed or the invitation was already redeemed, **then** it cannot activate an account and a safe expiry/reuse message is shown.
12. **Given** a pending invitation that was lost or expired, **when** an authorized tenant manager or permitted Super Admin selects Resend Invitation, **then** the system sends a replacement invitation to that same invited email with a fresh seven-day (168-hour) validity period; the previous invitation becomes unusable immediately, including when its original expiry has not yet been reached.
13. **Given** an unauthorized user, or the invitee acting without administrative permissions, **when** they attempt to resend an invitation, **then** the request is denied. No invitation reminder or self-service reissue action is offered in the MVP.
13a. **Given** a pending, unredeemed staff invitation, **when** an employee, tenant manager, permitted Super Admin or API client attempts to manually cancel it without reissuing, **then** no standalone cancellation is offered or authorized in the MVP; the link can become unusable only through its seven-day expiry, successful single-use redemption, or valid administrator-initiated replacement that invalidates older links. **Given** a mistakenly sent invitation, **when** no replacement is issued, **then** it remains valid until one of those normal invalidation conditions occurs; cancellation controls and bypass endpoints MUST NOT be introduced implicitly.
13b. **Given** a staff invitation was successfully issued or resent for a pending tenant-local invitee, **when** an otherwise-authorized manager tries Resend Invitation less than 15 minutes (900 seconds) after the latest successful issuance for that pending invitation, **then** no replacement message or token is issued, the original invitation and its original expiry remain unchanged, and the rejected attempt does not restart the cooldown. **When** the manager retries at or after 900 seconds, **then** the resend MAY issue a new single-use seven-day link and atomically invalidate all earlier links. Concurrent resend attempts MUST NOT evade the cooldown; another tenant's independent invitation for the same email MUST not share the cooldown.
13c. **Given** five successful **resends** (not including initial issuance) for the same pending tenant invitation during the preceding rolling 24 hours, **when** an authorized administrator attempts a sixth resend, **then** no new invitation or email is issued; no older link is invalidated, and the denied attempt is not counted. **When** the oldest of those resends falls outside the rolling 24-hour period and the 15-minute interval has also elapsed, **then** an otherwise-authorized resend MAY succeed. Parallel resend requests MUST NOT exceed the five-resend limit, and identical email addresses used for independent invitations in different tenants MUST have independent counters.
14. **Given** an invitee or existing employee is setting or changing a username, **when** they submit Arabic/non-Latin characters, numbers, spaces, hyphens, or any character outside ASCII letters (`A-Z`, `a-z`), underscore (`_`) and dot (`.`), **then** a localized validation error is shown. Names such as `ahmed`, `ahmed_ali`, and `ahmed.ali` pass the full character/placement and **3–30-character inclusive length** checks, subject to tenant uniqueness.
15. **Given** an invitee or existing employee chooses a username, **when** it has 2 or 31 characters, **then** it is rejected; **when** it has exactly 3 or 30 permitted characters, **then** it passes length validation, subject to all other approved username checks.
16. **Given** tenant A already has a staff username `Ahmed`, **when** another account tries to register or change its username to `ahmed` or `AHMED`, **then** the operation is rejected as a tenant-local duplicate. **When** the same account signs in using any of those capitalizations with its correct password, **then** the sign-in succeeds for that tenant. An identical username in a different tenant remains allowed.
17. **Given** a candidate username contains a number (e.g. `ahmed123`), **when** the staff member submits it during invitation activation or username change, **then** it is rejected; **given** names `ahmed_ali` and `ahmed.ali`, **when** checked against the approved full format, **then** both are valid subject to tenant uniqueness.
18. **Given** an invitee or employee submits `.ahmed`, `_ahmed`, `ahmed.`, `ahmed_`, `ahmed..ali`, `ahmed__ali`, or `ahmed._ali`, **when** the username is validated during activation or change, **then** the system rejects it with a localized explanation because a dot/underscore may only separate English-letter sequences.
19. **Given** an activated staff account with a verified email, **when** the employee, tenant manager, permitted platform administrator or an API client attempts to replace the registered email, **then** the update is refused regardless of role. The email remains readable as an account attribute, remains unchanged for sign-in and recovery, and any other authorized staff-profile edits remain possible.
20. **Given** an employee permanently loses access to their registered email, **when** an authorized tenant manager handles the replacement, **then** the manager disables the original account, invalidates its access, and sends a normal seven-day tenant-bound invitation to the new email for a separately created account with a new immutable account identifier. The original account's email, historical data and audit trail remain unchanged.
21. **Given** a replacement account is activated after lost-mailbox re-onboarding, **when** it begins working, **then** previous record ownership, assignments and historical author identities are not automatically transferred or rewritten; any permitted handover requires a separately authorized and auditable administrative action.
22. **Given** an authorized manager invites a replacement after permanent mailbox loss, **when** assigning roles to the new staff account, **then** the manager must choose only roles they are permitted to grant, from scratch; roles from the disabled account MUST NOT be copied or preselected.
23. **Given** a staff member requests password-change/reset help, **when** an authorized tenant manager initiates a reset for that employee, **then** the manager MUST enter and securely verify **the manager's own current password** before the reset can be initiated; no manager OTP/MFA is required. The system initiates a secure, tenant-bound recovery flow allowing the **employee** to choose a new password privately; the manager cannot see, choose, enter or retrieve the employee's existing or new password. Only the intended staff account is targeted.
24. **Given** the manager enters an incorrect password, lacks reset permission, or targets an employee in another tenant, **when** they attempt to initiate a reset, **then** the action is denied without issuing a recovery capability. **Given** a properly verified, authorized manager starts a reset, **when** the employee securely completes it, **then** all previous sessions for that employee are invalidated, and the operation is auditable.
25. **Given** a staff password-reset link initiated either through self-service verified-email recovery or manager-assisted recovery, **when** the intended staff member redeems it less than 60 minutes after issuance, **then** it may be used once to set a new password if every tenant, account, and security check passes; **when** the link is already redeemed or 60 minutes have elapsed, **then** it must no longer authorize a password change, even if the email remains available. Expiry and replay failures must not disclose sensitive account information.
26. **Given** an unexpired, unused password-reset link already exists for a staff account, **when** a second reset link is successfully issued for that same tenant account by either self-service recovery or an authorized manager, **then** all earlier reset links for that account become invalid immediately, the new link has a fresh 60-minute validity from issuance, and only the newest link may be redeemed once. An attempted simultaneous redemption/reissue MUST NOT allow an earlier link to succeed after the newer one has been issued.
27. **Given** a password-reset link has been issued for one tenant staff account, **when** another self-service or manager-assisted link request targets that account less than five minutes (300 seconds) after the accepted issuance, **then** no replacement link is issued and any existing valid link remains valid. At or after the five-minute threshold a further otherwise-authorized request MAY issue a fresh 60-minute single-use link, immediately invalidating older links. Concurrent requests MUST not bypass the five-minute per-account cooldown; requests for independent accounts, including another tenant account with the same email, have independent cooldowns. Self-service responses MUST not disclose whether the target account exists or has been rate-limited.
28. **Given** a tenant staff account has already been issued five password-reset links in the preceding rolling 24 hours, counting both employee self-service and manager-assisted issuances, **when** any party attempts a sixth issuance, **then** no link is issued, no earlier link is invalidated, and the response does not expose account existence. **When** the oldest counted issuance falls outside the rolling 24-hour window and the five-minute cooldown has elapsed, **then** an otherwise authorized issuance may proceed. Rejected requests MUST NOT count toward the five-link limit, and concurrent requests MUST NOT exceed it.

### User Story 3 — Staff use long-lived, controllable sessions on multiple devices (Priority: P1)

As a staff member, I need to keep working across browser restarts and multiple devices, inspect and revoke my sessions, and have lost or compromised access invalidated promptly.

**Why this priority:** Rolling 30-day persistence and immediate revocation are explicit project-owner security and UX choices.

**Independent Test:** Open sessions on two devices, record meaningful activity, change and recover a password, revoke one session, and suspend the tenant; assert expected authorization behavior in every case.

**Acceptance Scenarios:**

1. **Given** a signed-in, active staff member, **when** they restart the browser before the 30-day inactivity period has elapsed, **then** the session remains usable subject to security checks.
2. **Given** a session with no verified meaningful user activity for 30 days, **when** any protected operation is attempted, **then** reauthentication is required, regardless of silent background token refreshes.
3. **Given** one account on multiple devices, **when** the user views active sessions and revokes a different one, **then** the revoked session can no longer perform protected operations while other valid sessions remain usable.
4. **Given** a successful password change during an active session, **when** other devices next request protected operations, **then** their prior sessions are denied; the current session remains only if safely revalidated.
5. **Given** email-based recovery or an administrative reset, **when** it completes, **then** all pre-reset sessions stop authorizing requests.
6. **Given** an account is disabled or its tenant is suspended, **when** any previously signed-in device makes a new protected request, **then** the request is rejected.

### User Story 4 — Tenant managers administer staff and constrained roles (Priority: P1)

As a tenant manager, I need to manage staff and reusable roles, assign multiple roles, and delegate narrow role-management authority without enabling privilege escalation or cross-tenant data access.

**Why this priority:** All later business modules must inherit centrally enforceable authorization.

**Independent Test:** Create two custom roles, assign both to one staff member, test union of allowed permissions, attempt self-elevation, attempt granting higher authority, and attempt cross-tenant operations.

**Acceptance Scenarios:**

1. **Given** an authorized tenant manager, **when** they invite, edit allowed administrative attributes (excluding the registered email after activation), disable or re-enable a staff account, **then** only staff within that tenant are affected and the action is audited.
2. **Given** an authorized role manager, **when** they create or change a tenant-local role, **then** the role contains only permissions within their permitted grant authority.
3. **Given** a staff member with two roles, **when** they perform a permitted action, **then** effective grants are evaluated using both roles, while applicable restrictions still block disallowed records or actions.
4. **Given** a delegated role manager, **when** they try to grant a permission above their authority or elevate themselves, **then** the operation is denied and the attempted privileged action is handled under security-event logging rules.
4d. **Given** a tenant staff member authorized for Role Management is assigned to an existing tenant-local role (including a tenant manager or a delegated role administrator), **when** they attempt to edit that same role's permissions, record scopes, name, description, or other role definition fields, **then** the edit is denied regardless of whether it would increase effective privileges. They MAY edit roles to which they are not assigned only if separately authorized under their grant authority. The self-membership prohibition MUST hold for role-edit APIs and concurrent role-membership changes; it MUST be checked against authoritative membership at commit time and logged as an unauthorized privileged attempt.
4e. **Given** a tenant-local role remains assigned to at least one staff account, **when** an otherwise-authorized role administrator attempts to remove/archive that role, **then** the operation MUST be rejected without changing the role or automatically removing it from any account. **Given** all staff-role assignments for that role have been explicitly removed by duly authorized administrators, **when** a role administrator separately authorized to retire it selects Archive Role, **then** the system MAY mark it archived only after a server-side concurrency-safe recheck confirms zero assignments. The role's identity and definition remain preserved, it disappears from active-role lists, and it cannot be assigned to employees while archived. A later authorized restoration is possible but MUST NOT automatically reinstate any former assignments. No cascading or implicit unassignment is permitted, including for disabled staff accounts; denied attempts, archival and future restoration MUST be auditable. Permanent role deletion is not part of the MVP.
4f. **Given** a tenant-local role is archived, **when** an authorized tenant manager or a delegated staff member explicitly granted `Restore Role` requests restoration, **then** the role MAY return to active status only when the actor has sufficient grant authority for every permission/scope the role would make available; restoration is tenant-bound, auditable, and restores no prior employee memberships. **Given** a staff member without explicit delegated `Restore Role` authority, or a request to restore a role containing privileges outside the actor's grant scope or protected tenant-manager authority, **when** restoration is attempted by UI or API, **then** deny the operation. Archived-role names MAY be reused by newly created active roles in the same tenant (CL-031). If restoration finds an active role with the same name, restoration MUST be blocked until a separately authorized rename of one of those roles produces a unique active name. No silent renaming, merging, replacing the newer role or automatic reassignment is permitted.
4a. **Given** any existing tenant manager or delegated role manager, **when** they attempt to appoint themselves or another employee as an additional tenant manager via employee invitation, role assignment, custom-role configuration or direct API/database operations, **then** protected tenant-manager authority is not granted. Only a properly authorized platform Super Admin can grant it; the denial and privileged grant are audited.
4b. **Given** a tenant manager, delegated role manager or ordinary employee, **when** they attempt to revoke/remove another user's protected tenant-manager authority through role changes, staff administration, direct API or other routes, **then** the attempt is denied. Only an authorized platform Super Admin can revoke that protected authority, and an active tenant cannot lose its last activated, active manager.
4c. **Given** a manager has been demoted by an authorized Super Admin, **when** the former manager signs in again to their unchanged employee account, **then** authorized access depends only on their remaining non-manager roles; removing Manager Role MUST NOT itself disable their account, erase audit attribution, replace their identity or alter their other role memberships. All sessions established before the demotion MUST already have been revoked across devices, with no old-token refresh or request accepted.
5. **Given** a staff member without permission to view an audit log, **when** they access the URL or underlying operation directly, **then** the operation is denied even if navigation links are hidden.
6. **Given** record-scope rules (All, Own, Assigned), **when** an authorization test uses representative foundation fixtures in two tenants, **then** a grant cannot bypass tenant isolation or an applicable record scope. Actual record ownership semantics are defined by each future domain spec.

### User Story 5 — Staff work inside an accessible, white-label bilingual shell (Priority: P1)

As a staff member, I need to recognize my organization on the login and workspace screens, use Arabic or English, choose the most comfortable theme, and navigate only areas I am allowed to use.

**Why this priority:** Accessibility, tenant-only identity, and shared UI foundations are required before business screens can ship.

**Independent Test:** In two tenants with different names/logos, exercise login and shell in Arabic/RTL and English/LTR, Dark/Light themes, keyboard-only operation, and permissions that hide unauthorized navigation.

**Acceptance Scenarios:**

1. **Given** a valid tenant subdomain, **when** the login page is opened, **then** it presents that tenant's name and optional logo, or a neutral icon when no logo exists, without software-provider branding.
2. **Given** an authorized tenant manager, **when** they change the organization name or upload, replace, or remove its logo, **then** only that tenant's authorized identity changes; staff and delegated role managers without this specific authority cannot do so.
3. **Given** an employee who changes language, **when** the interface renders in Arabic or English, **then** translated UI uses correct RTL/LTR direction without altering identifiers or business records.
4. **Given** a user with theme set to System, **when** their device preference changes, **then** the UI follows it; with explicit Dark or Light selection, the selected preference persists across visits.
5. **Given** different tenant logos, **when** each tenant's portal renders, **then** the same fixed accessible colors are used, with no tenant color picker or default ERAK artwork for other tenants.
6. **Given** a module without nested sections, **when** staff open it, **then** the content uses the available width without an empty secondary sidebar; modules requiring subdivisions may show a secondary sidebar.
7. **Given** keyboard-only and screen-reader use, **when** the user traverses interactive shell and forms, **then** focus, labels, errors and contrast meet the approved accessibility standard.

### User Story 6 — Authorized staff review important administrative history (Priority: P1)

As a tenant manager or explicitly permitted auditor, I need to inspect administrative and important mutation history within my allowed tenant scope, while other users cannot alter or purge the evidence.

**Why this priority:** Role changes, suspensions, invitations and sensitive settings must be accountable from the beginning.

**Independent Test:** Perform administrative changes in two tenants and platform administration; confirm actor/time/scope/event coverage, authorized viewing, no cross-tenant visibility, and tamper protection.

**Acceptance Scenarios:**

1. **Given** staff/roles/membership, tenant-lifecycle, settings, or other in-scope important operations, **when** they are performed, **then** audit events include actor, action, time, tenant/context where applicable, and safe resource identifiers.
2. **Given** a user with explicit audit_logs.view authorization, **when** they review the log, **then** they see only events permitted for their tenant and audit visibility scope.
2a. **Given** a delegated employee holds `audit_logs.view` scoped to staff- and role-management event categories, **when** they view, filter, search or export their tenant audit log, **then** only those explicitly allowed categories are returned. Settings, unrelated business, platform and other-tenant events remain inaccessible through both UI and API, including details and result counts; a generic `audit_logs.view` grant without authorized categories MUST NOT provide unrestricted viewing.
3. **Given** an ordinary staff member, **when** they attempt to edit or manually delete an audit event, **then** the operation is blocked.
4. **Given** the platform Super Admin, **when** they view platform administrative history, **then** it does not automatically disclose tenant-private audit contents.
5. **Given** a user merely opens a beneficiary/donor record in a future domain, **when** no significant action occurs, **then** the foundation does not automatically create a dedicated read-only business audit event.

### User Story 7 — Operators can release and recover the platform safely (Priority: P1; launch gate)

As the responsible operator, I need separate non-production and production environments, a reviewable rollout, regular recoverable database and file backups, and evidence that tenant isolation survives release changes.

**Why this priority:** A system holding personal and potentially financial information must not launch without verified recoverability.

**Independent Test:** Complete a documented non-production deployment/migration and a rehearsed restore in isolation, including required stored files, then demonstrate the release gate blocks production without evidence.

**Acceptance Scenarios:**

1. **Given** a pending production release, **when** mandatory tests, migration review or recovery evidence are missing, **then** the release is not accepted as ready.
2. **Given** separate Development and Production environments, **when** development activity or tests run, **then** they cannot operate on Production accounts, storage or private records.
3. **Given** a completed regular Production backup, **when** operators perform an approved isolated restore test, **then** the database and in-scope stored file contents can be recovered and verified.
4. **Given** a shared multi-tenant Production database, **when** restore is planned, **then** the plan explicitly considers impact on all tenants and does not silently replace the live database.
5. **Given** a tenant A test user, **when** they attempt tenant B reads, writes, references, exports, file access or suspended-tenant operations, **then** all prohibited cases are rejected.

### Edge Cases

- Unknown, malformed, reserved or duplicate subdomain; hostname spoofing and tenant-mismatched invitation links.
- One email associated with separate tenants and potentially multiple pending invitations; invitation reused, expired, already activated or resent; concurrent username reservation/activation.
- Invitation activation attempted immediately before or at/after the seven-day expiration boundary; the invitation stays non-redeemable after expiry even if a related email message is still available.
- Reissue requested just before activation, two reissues requested concurrently, or activation attempted using the original link after reissue; only the latest valid invitation can activate an account once, with tenant-scoped authorization and no duplicate staff identity. Resend attempts before 15 minutes from last successful issuance/reissue are rejected without sending email, changing the original expiry or revoking the previous link; exactly 15 minutes is permitted subject to authorization. Concurrent requests must not bypass the cooldown or five-successful-resends-per-rolling-24-hours cap. Initial invitation issuance is not itself a resend and does not consume one of those five slots. Denied sends must not change the current link or counters.
- Pending invite accidentally sent to an unwanted address: no separate manual cancel flow or API is available in the MVP; only seven-day expiry, one-time redemption, or authorized reissue invalidating earlier links can end the link's validity. Direct cancellation attempts must be denied.
- Simultaneous username changes to the same value, attempts to change another user's username, old-username sign-in after a change, and expired or revoked sessions attempting an update.
- Username attempts containing Arabic script, digits, whitespace, hyphens, visually similar non-ASCII letters, emoji or characters outside `[A-Za-z_.]`; the same character-set validation MUST apply during activation and username changes. The 3–30-character inclusive boundary MUST hold for initial selection and subsequent changes. Username uniqueness and sign-in MUST be case-insensitive within the tenant (e.g., `Ahmed` = `ahmed` = `AHMED`); simultaneous case-variant registrations/renames MUST not create duplicates. Punctuation cannot appear at the beginning or end, and consecutive or mixed adjacent punctuation is prohibited; dots and underscores may separate English-letter sequences only.
- Same verified email entered twice within one tenant: reject duplicate account/invitation safely; that same email may still be used by a different tenant.
- Attempt to change an activated employee email from self-service settings, tenant administration, platform administration, a direct API call or a lower-level Auth update; application permissions MUST NOT accidentally override the immutability rule. If mailbox access is permanently lost, the approved replacement is disabling the old account and inviting a distinct new account at a new email (CL-013), never changing the old email.
- Tenant suspended during an active browser session, email recovery, or a pending invite; later reactivation without accidental privilege restoration to a disabled individual account.
- Lost-email account re-onboarding attempted by an unauthorized employee or manager, attempts to reuse the old account's internal ID or rewrite old audit author IDs, and continuing to use sessions from the disabled old account. Recovery/replacement MUST not migrate historical authorship, ownership or role memberships by default; the manager must select permitted new roles explicitly.
- Two simultaneous role updates or a role removal while a staff member remains signed in; effective privileges must not persist improperly. A tenant role administrator attempting to edit a role to which they themselves belong (including permission reductions, naming changes and concurrent membership/edit races) MUST be denied; UI hiding alone does not satisfy server-side authorization. Archived role names MAY be reused, but an archived role cannot be restored while another active role has the same name; an explicitly authorized rename MUST first eliminate the conflict, with atomic uniqueness checks and without modifying role identity or employee memberships. Attempted archival of a role assigned to any employee (including disabled accounts) MUST fail without cascade. Atomic assignment/archive checks must prevent archival when an assignment is added concurrently; no staff assignments are silently removed. An archived role is excluded from active role pickers and cannot be assigned via UI or API; only an authorized restoration can return it to active status, without restoring former employee memberships.
- Attempts to use a role's grant permission to modify itself or protected tenant-manager/platform authority; no tenant-level role edit, invitation or direct API can mint or remove protected tenant-manager authority. Only an authorized platform Super Admin can grant or revoke manager authority, without default access to tenant-private data. Concurrent revocations and manager disablements must not leave an active tenant without at least one activated, active manager; pending invitations do not count.
- User has several roles with overlapping grants but stricter applicable scope; absence of permission continues to deny.
- Session revoked on one device while another refreshes its token; background refresh must not reset user inactivity without actual activity. Manager-role revocation while signed in on multiple devices MUST revoke all pre-demotion sessions and their refresh capability, including racing refresh/protected API requests; the employee can only resume through new login with remaining non-manager roles.
- Email account enumeration through tenant-scoped login/recovery, and credential stuffing/abuse.
- Password-reset redemption immediately before, at, or after the 60-minute expiry boundary; expired and redeemed links MUST be rejected even if a provider token/session remains technically valid. Successful issuance of any newer reset link MUST immediately invalidate every previous unused link for that same tenant staff account, even if it is unexpired, across self-service and manager-initiated paths; concurrent issuance/redemption MUST be enforced atomically. A five-minute (300-second) minimum interval between successful reset-link issuances is required per tenant staff account across both recovery paths, with atomic enforcement; a rejected cooldown request MUST NOT invalidate the current reset link. A maximum of five successfully issued reset links per rolling 24-hour window is also required per tenant staff account, including both request paths. Rejected issuance attempts do not count or invalidate earlier links. IP, global abuse and delivery controls remain to be clarified.
- Missing logo, unsupported/corrupt/oversized logo, absent organization name, or accidental display of another organization's logo.
- Arabic-English mixed text and email/usernames in RTL screens, missing translations, inaccessible brand images, and theme preference before account activation.
- Audit viewer loses permission between page load and export; audit contents must not leak through stale client state.
- Interrupted backup, backup unavailable under the purchased service plan, backup containing private files without encryption, and failed restore rehearsal.

## Requirements (mandatory)

### Functional Requirements

**Tenant provisioning and administrative separation**

- **FR-001:** The platform MUST expose separate Super Admin and tenant staff experiences with independent authorization boundaries.
- **FR-002:** Only an authorized platform Super Admin MUST be able to create a tenant; public tenant signup MUST NOT exist.
- **FR-003:** Tenant creation MUST require an organization display name, a unique valid subdomain, an activation state, and an initial tenant-manager email invitation; logo MUST be optional.
- **FR-004:** Each tenant MUST be identified at sign-in by a trusted lookup of its subdomain; a caller-supplied tenant identifier MUST NOT establish its authorization context.
- **FR-005:** A tenant subdomain MUST remain immutable after tenant creation; custom domains MUST NOT be offered in the MVP.
- **FR-006:** The platform MUST support activation and suspension of tenants while preserving tenant records; suspension MUST deny new and existing-session protected operations.
- **FR-007:** Tenant deletion, subscription-plan configuration, expiry-based access and platform billing MUST NOT be offered in the MVP.

**Invitations and authentication**

- **FR-008:** Only appropriately authorized administrators MUST be able to invite, disable and manage tenant staff accounts. **Only a platform Super Admin with the relevant platform authority may appoint or grant protected tenant-manager authority to any additional manager**, whether during invitation, promotion of an existing tenant employee, or other role-assignment flow. An existing tenant manager or delegated tenant role administrator MUST NOT confer that protected authority. This is an authorization rule, not a grant of default access to private beneficiary/donor/financial records for platform administrators (see FR-029). **Only a platform Super Admin with the relevant authority may revoke/remove protected tenant-manager authority (CL-024)**, whether by role removal, demotion or administrative staff changes; tenant managers and delegated role administrators MUST NOT revoke that authority via any path. Revocation MUST be auditable and immediately affect protected authorization checks. An active tenant MUST retain at least one activated, active tenant manager: attempts to revoke the last such manager MUST be denied until another active manager is established, including under concurrent requests. **Manager demotion MUST remove only protected tenant-manager authority, without disabling the employee account or changing any other existing role memberships (CL-025).** The account retains the same tenant staff ID, immutable email and historical audit authorship. All manager-only authorizations MUST be denied immediately after demotion, including requests from sessions created before the role removal; remaining role grants continue under the existing security and tenant checks. If the person has no other roles, remaining enabled does not grant new protected permissions. **Immediately upon protected Manager Role demotion, ALL existing sessions of the affected tenant staff account MUST be revoked across all devices (CL-026)**, and pre-demotion session/refresh tokens MUST no longer authorize protected operations or renew access. This MUST be enforced server-side even if an old browser still holds tokens, be safe against concurrent requests, and be audited without exposing token values. The employee MUST be able to log in again with existing credentials and exercise only their unchanged non-manager roles; no change to enabled status or password is implied.
- **FR-009:** Staff onboarding MUST begin with a one-time, tenant-bound verified-email invitation valid for **seven days (168 hours) from issuance**. A recipient MUST be able to set their own username and password only while the invitation is valid and unredeemed. At or after its expiry or after successful redemption, the invitation MUST no longer authorize account activation. **Only an authorized tenant manager or permitted Super Admin** MAY manually select **Resend Invitation** for a pending invite that was lost or expired; this MUST issue and send a replacement to the invitee's email with a fresh seven-day (168-hour) validity period and MUST immediately invalidate every previous invitation link for the same pending account, including unexpired links. Reissue MUST not produce duplicate staff accounts and MUST be tenant-scoped, concurrency-safe and appropriately audited. **Resend Invitation MUST be subject to a minimum 15-minute (900-second) interval from the latest successful initial issue or resend of the same pending tenant invitation.** A too-early resend MUST issue no new link or email, MUST NOT invalidate the current link or alter its existing expiry, and MUST NOT reset the timer. At or after 900 seconds a valid, authorized resend may issue a fresh seven-day invitation, invalidating previous links in accordance with CL-004. The minimum interval MUST be checked server-side with concurrency-safe enforcement and tenant-local pending-invitation identity (not email globally). **No more than five successful Resend Invitation operations MAY occur for the same pending tenant invitation during any rolling 24-hour period**, counted across all authorized administrators. Initial invitation issuance does not count as a resend. Any sixth attempt in the rolling window MUST issue no link/email and MUST NOT invalidate, alter or extend the current invitation; rejected attempts MUST NOT count or change the cooldown. When an earlier resend exits the rolling window, issuance MAY proceed if the 15-minute cooldown and authorization requirements also pass. The window and counters MUST be tenant-local and concurrency-safe even with parallel administrators and addresses shared across tenants. Self-service reissue by invitees and automatic reminder emails are NOT MVP requirements. **Standalone manual invitation cancellation MUST NOT be available** to tenant managers, Super Admins, invitees, or API clients in the MVP. A pending invitation may cease to be usable only through the approved seven-day expiry, successful one-time redemption, or an authorized administrator's reissue (which invalidates all former links); cancellation is not a separate invitation-management operation. The mechanism MUST be compatible with a security-reviewed Supabase Auth flow; default provider link lifetimes MUST NOT be presumed sufficient.
- **FR-010:** A staff username AND registered verified email MUST each be unique within its tenant, and MAY repeat in other tenants. Two staff accounts in the same tenant MUST NOT share a verified email. The **only permitted username characters** MUST be ASCII English letters (`A-Z`, `a-z`), underscore (`_`), and dot (`.`). Usernames MUST contain English letters; **digits (`0-9`)**, Arabic or other Unicode letters, whitespace, hyphens, and other punctuation MUST be rejected. This **supersedes the previous digits-and-underscore-only character rule (CL-006)**. Validate the new character set consistently during invitation activation and subsequent username changes. Username length MUST be **3–30 characters inclusive** for both account activation and subsequent changes. Username matching, uniqueness and login MUST be **case-insensitive within a tenant**: for example, `Ahmed`, `ahmed` and `AHMED` are the same username, and no two staff accounts in one tenant may claim case variants. This MUST be enforced server-side with concurrency-safe uniqueness, including during username changes. Whether entered capitalization is preserved for display is an implementation detail; it MUST NOT affect identity or authorization. Usernames MUST begin and end with English ASCII letters, and each dot (`.`) or underscore (`_`) MUST occur only between English-letter sequences; no leading, trailing, consecutive, or mixed-adjacent punctuation is valid. Together with the 3–30-character length limit, the accepted syntax is equivalent to `^[A-Za-z]+(?:[._][A-Za-z]+)*$` (illustrative expression, not a required implementation method).
- **Email immutability clarification (CL-012; part of FR-010 and staff account lifecycle):** Once a staff account has been activated, its registered email MUST remain immutable. Neither the employee nor a tenant manager nor a platform Super Admin MAY modify it, including through account settings, administrative workflows, public/server APIs or the underlying Auth integration. Application-controlled operations MUST enforce this rule on the server, while permitting the email to be displayed and used for sign-in/password recovery. Permanent loss of access to the original mailbox is handled only through disabled-old-account and new-invitation re-onboarding (CL-013); this MUST NOT mutate the original email. Pending invitation cancellation/replacement semantics remain separately undecided; administrator-triggered resend under CL-004 targets the same invited address.
- **FR-011:** Staff MUST be able to change their own tenant-local username from account settings with their existing, currently valid authenticated session, **without a separate identity-verification challenge** (password re-entry, email code, or reauthentication solely for this change). The server MUST check that the session is valid and the account belongs to the resolved tenant and the requesting employee; invalid, expired, revoked, cross-tenant, and other-account requests MUST be denied. The requested username MUST be available in the same tenant; changes MUST be validated server-side, concurrency-safe, and auditable. Immutable account identity, email, roles and records MUST NOT be replaced by a username change.
- **FR-012:** Tenant sign-in MUST accept either a tenant-local username or the registered email plus the account's password. Username lookup MUST be case-insensitive within the resolved tenant; the entered capitalization MUST NOT change which tenant account is authenticated.
- **FR-013:** The same real email MUST be supported by independent staff accounts in different tenants, without requiring shared passwords or cross-tenant identity switching. **Implementation blocked by G-01 until a security-reviewed working proof exists.**
- **FR-014:** Authentication and recovery MUST not reveal account existence or other tenant data through untrusted lookup, errors, logs or URLs.
- **FR-015:** Staff MUST be able to recover access through their verified email. A staff member MAY ask their organization's authorized tenant manager to initiate a password reset. To authorize this action, the manager MUST enter **their own current account password** and the system MUST verify it securely against the authoritative authentication service (not application-stored passwords); a valid session alone is insufficient for manager-initiated reset. Incorrect password, unauthorized manager, wrong-tenant target, and disabled-account cases MUST deny initiation. No manager OTP/MFA or employee-password entry is required. After successful authorization, the system MUST initiate a secure, tenant-bound, single-use recovery process that allows **the employee alone** to choose their new password without disclosing it to the manager; administrator MUST NOT know or choose the employee's existing or new password or impersonate them. Manager confirmation attempts and reset issuance/completion MUST be appropriately audited without logging passwords or tokens; completed administrative recovery MUST invalidate ALL employee sessions predating the reset, under FR-021. Password-reset links/tokens, whether issued through self-service verified-email recovery or manager-assisted recovery, MUST have a **60-minute validity period from issuance** and be **single-use**. Redemption at or after expiry or after first successful use MUST be rejected, enforced authoritatively server-side and independent of any longer underlying provider token lifetime. **Each successfully issued replacement reset link MUST immediately invalidate all older links for the same tenant staff account**, including links not yet expired or redeemed; this MUST apply across self-service and manager-initiated reset issuance, with concurrency-safe server-side validation so only the latest issued link remains eligible to be redeemed. A replacement receives its own new 60-minute validity period from issuance. The system MUST allow **no more than one successfully issued password-reset link per tenant staff account in any 5-minute (300-second) interval**, across self-service and manager-assisted paths. The cooldown begins when a link is issued; an attempt before 300 seconds MUST not send a link, reset the cooldown, or invalidate the prior link. At or after 300 seconds a valid new issuance follows CL-017 and invalidates older links. Across the same tenant staff identity, the system MUST also issue **at most 5 password-reset links within any rolling 24-hour window**, combining self-service and manager-assisted issuances. A sixth attempted issuance inside that rolling window MUST be denied, MUST NOT invalidate an already issued link, and MUST NOT count as an issuance; when the oldest issuance falls outside the rolling window, a new request MAY succeed if the five-minute cooldown and other checks pass. Both limits MUST be enforced server-side and concurrency-safely, using tenant-bound immutable staff identity rather than a global email address. Public recovery responses MUST avoid revealing account existence or throttling status. Delivery rules and additional IP/global abuse limits beyond these account-local controls remain to be defined in G-02; this contract, including provider compatibility, MUST be demonstrated under G-01. If an employee permanently loses access to that email, this MUST NOT be treated as email mutation or a password reset bypass: an authorized tenant manager MUST disable their original tenant account before inviting a **separate new account** at a different email using the normal secure seven-day invitation process. Disabling the original account MUST deny all of its existing sessions under FR-022. The replacement account MUST receive a new internal identity and roles explicitly chosen by the authorized manager under grant-authority checks, with NO automatic copying or preselection from the old account; the old account, original immutable email, historical records and audit authorship MUST remain preserved. Record ownership or task/responsibility transfers MUST NOT be automatic and MUST require separately authorized, auditable administrative procedures; exact handover policies will be specified with the relevant business domains.
- **FR-016:** Staff self-registration, donor/beneficiary/volunteer sign-in, SMS recovery and MFA/2FA MUST NOT be introduced in this MVP.
- **FR-017:** Credentials, privileged secrets and raw passwords MUST NOT be exposed in logs or application-owned credential tables, or sent to untrusted parties. User-supplied passwords may only pass through the protected input/authentication path required for the manager's own-password confirmation and the employee's own new-password setting; no client may retrieve other users' passwords or privileged secrets.

**Sessions**

- **FR-018:** A valid session MUST survive browser restart and MUST expire after 30 days without verified meaningful user activity; background-only refresh MUST NOT count as activity.
- **FR-019:** A staff member MUST be allowed concurrent sessions across multiple devices without an MVP device-count cap.
- **FR-020:** A staff member MUST be able to view and revoke individual other sessions and revoke all other sessions from account settings.
- **FR-021:** Changing a password MUST invalidate other sessions, retaining the current session only if safely revalidated; recovery or admin reset MUST invalidate all pre-reset sessions.
- **FR-022:** Tenant suspension, employee disablement, and session revocation MUST be enforced on protected operations even if old browser tokens remain locally present. Revocation of protected tenant-manager authority (CL-026) MUST invalidate **all pre-demotion sessions and their refresh capability across all devices** at the time the role revocation commits; future protected operations MUST reject those sessions, including concurrent token refresh attempts. The still-enabled employee may create new sessions by authenticating again and has access only to remaining ordinary roles (CL-025).

**Roles, permissions, and record scope**

- **FR-023:** Each tenant MUST be able to create and manage its own roles and staff-role assignments; a staff account MUST support multiple simultaneous roles. **No tenant staff actor with Role Management authority, including a tenant manager or delegated role administrator, may modify a role to which that actor is currently assigned (CL-027)**. This rule applies even to non-escalating edits such as changing permissions, scopes, display name or description; a different duly authorized administrator who is not assigned to that role must perform the edit. Every role-definition mutation MUST check the acting staff account's authoritative role membership and grant authority on the server, safely against concurrent changes. Role-manager authorization does not grant a right to bypass this restriction via direct APIs or other clients; attempted unauthorized edits MUST be auditable. **Deletion of any tenant-local role currently assigned to one or more staff accounts MUST be refused (CL-028)**, including when only disabled staff accounts hold it. Each staff-role assignment MUST be explicitly removed beforehand in a separately authorized and auditable operation, respecting tenant/grant authority. Deletion MUST NOT cascade, automatically unassign employees or be authorized solely because a UI button is visible. The server MUST atomically verify zero role assignments at deletion time, preventing concurrent assignment/deletion races, and log denial and successful deletion events. This does not override protected tenant-manager-role controls. **Retiring an unassigned tenant role MUST use reversible archival (CL-029), not permanent deletion.** Once a role has no assignments, an authorized archive operation marks it as archived while preserving its stable identity, definition and audit/history references; archived roles MUST NOT appear among active assignable roles or be assignable through any UI/API while archived. A role MAY be restored through a separately authorized and audited operation, subject to the same tenant and protected-authority checks; restoration MUST NOT automatically reassign the role to former members or grant permissions. The archive operation MUST atomically ensure zero current staff assignments (CL-028), and no permanent deletion of tenant role definitions is offered in the MVP. **Restore Role authority (CL-030):** A tenant manager MAY restore an archived tenant-local role within their grant authority; a delegated staff member MAY restore it ONLY if explicitly assigned the `Restore Role` permission and independently authorized to grant all permissions/scopes contained in that role. Grant checks MUST be performed server-side against the archived role's complete current definition at restoration time, to prevent an indirect privilege escalation, including protected tenant-manager authority. Authorized restoration MUST retain the same role identity/definition, audit the action, and MUST NOT reinstate former staff-role memberships or automatically grant privileges. Neither possessing general Role Management nor being able to archive a role automatically grants a delegate the right to restore it. Roles from another tenant cannot be restored. **Role-name reuse and restoration collision policy (CL-031):** Archiving a role releases its name for reuse by a separately created tenant-local active role, without changing the archived role's stable identity, grants or audit history. Active roles within a tenant MUST have distinct names; archived roles MAY share a name with an active role. Restoring an archived role MUST NOT cause two active roles to have the same name: if its original name is already used by an active role, the actor MUST first arrange an explicitly authorized rename of the active role or the archived role as part of a separately authorized restoration workflow. The system MUST reject any unresolved name conflict with a clear validation message and no partial restoration, automatically renamed role, overwritten role, reassigned employee or merged permissions. Name uniqueness and archive/restore/rename race conditions MUST be enforced server-side and atomically within a tenant. The exact text normalization and case-sensitivity policy for role names is a technical validation detail to be specified consistently; no rule is inferred here.
- **FR-024:** Effective grants MUST combine the grants of assigned roles without bypassing explicit record restrictions, tenant bounds, or higher-priority prohibitions.
- **FR-025:** Authorization MUST deny by default and MUST be checked for protected operations, not only screen or navigation visibility.
- **FR-026:** Tenant managers MAY delegate role administration to authorized staff, but delegates MUST NOT grant privileges beyond their authorized authority, self-elevate, modify a role of which they themselves are a member (CL-027), archive a role still assigned to any staff account (CL-028), assign an archived role without authorized restoration (CL-029), restore an archived role without explicit delegated `Restore Role` authority or beyond the actor's permission grant scope (CL-030), grant protected tenant-manager authority (including to themselves or another employee), or grant platform-administrator privileges. **Tenant-manager authority is protected and non-grantable at tenant level**, regardless of role names, UI visibility, custom-role permission edits, APIs, or indirect assignment paths; it may be newly granted only by an authorized platform Super Admin (CL-023). Protected manager authority may be revoked **only by an authorized platform Super Admin** (CL-024), never through tenant-local role delegation, staff editing or API operations; at least one active manager must remain in an active tenant. Upon Manager Role revocation, the staff account MUST remain enabled, retain all previously assigned non-manager roles, and immediately lose protected manager privileges (CL-025), without altering its identity or audit history. No replacement default role is automatically assigned.
- **FR-027:** The foundation MUST establish the permission vocabulary and extensibility for relevant create/view/update/archive/restore/export/approval and administrative actions without inventing domain-level business behavior.
- **FR-028:** The authorization foundation MUST support the All / Own / Assigned record scopes where domain specs call for them. Ownership, assignment and applicable operations MUST be defined by each future module.
- **FR-029:** The platform Super Admin MUST NOT gain default permission to inspect beneficiary/donor personal details, tenant files or financial data solely through platform administrative status.

**Audit and privacy**

- **FR-030:** The foundation MUST record important tenant lifecycle, account, membership, role, permission, sensitive settings and in-scope business mutation events with safe actor/action/time/context metadata.
- **FR-031:** Viewing tenant audit logs MUST require explicit tenant-scoped `audit_logs.view` permission and must enforce its applicable visibility scope; platform administrative logs MUST remain separate. **Delegated audit viewers MUST see only explicitly granted event categories (CL-032)**, such as staff, role and tenant-settings events; `audit_logs.view` alone MUST NOT expose all tenant events. Enforce tenant/category authorization server-side for lists, details, filtering, search, counts, pagination and exports, including direct API requests and permission changes during a session. Audit access MUST NOT reveal platform logs, another tenant's events, raw donor/beneficiary data, secrets, or other sensitive records. An authorized tenant manager MAY be permitted to view all tenant administrative audit categories within the same restrictions. Exact event-category catalog and field-redaction rules remain for technical planning.
- **FR-032:** Ordinary staff/tenant managers MUST NOT be able to modify or manually erase audit records. No indiscriminate read-only business audit event stream MUST be imposed.
- **FR-033:** Audit event retention MUST follow an approved per-category retention policy established before production release; exact durations MUST NOT be invented in this spec.
- **FR-034:** Any tenant-private file access and exported records MUST remain tenant/permission scoped, including when a staff member owns or is assigned a record.

**White-label identity, language, and shell**

- **FR-035:** The portal MUST show only the active organization's required name and optional logo; without a logo, it MUST show a neutral accessible icon and the organization name.
- **FR-036:** Only the tenant manager (or authorized Super Admin intervention) MUST be able to modify a tenant's display name and logo; such changes MUST be validated and audited.
- **FR-037:** All tenants MUST use the same fixed, accessible color palette; tenant-selected colors and provider branding MUST NOT appear in tenant-facing surfaces, assets, notifications or document metadata.
- **FR-038:** Arabic/RTL MUST be the initial language/direction and English/LTR MUST be supported in the first release across all shipped experiences.
- **FR-039:** Employees MUST be able to choose Dark, Light or System theme; System MUST initially follow device preference, and user selections MUST persist.
- **FR-040:** The staff portal MUST provide permission-aware primary navigation and allow modules to omit a secondary sidebar. The foundation MUST NOT fill the shell with unapproved business dashboards or copy reference mockups literally.
- **FR-041:** Shipped interactive UI MUST meet WCAG 2.2 AA objectives, keyboard navigation, screen-reader feedback, responsive behavior, and clear mixed-direction content.
- **FR-042:** Common visual controls in both applications MUST follow the shared presentation system required by the constitution, with no tenant-specific copied component variants.

**Production readiness**

- **FR-043:** Development and Production MUST be isolated persistent environments. Tests MUST NOT access Production private data without separately approved controls.
- **FR-044:** Before Production handles live data, the platform MUST have verified regular automated database backups targeting at least daily recovery points and separate appropriate protection/restoration of required stored files.
- **FR-045:** Backup retention, recovery ownership/objectives, release rollback, audit retention, and restore rehearsal frequency MUST be documented and approved before Production launch.
- **FR-046:** Acceptance testing MUST include two tenants, positive and negative permission/security cases, cross-tenant access attempts, active-session suspension/revocation, and translation/theme/accessibility checks.

### Key Entities (conceptual; NOT a physical schema)

- **Tenant (organization):** Required display name, immutable subdomain, active/suspended state, optional logo, and administrative lifecycle.
- **Platform Administrator:** A privileged platform-level identity for creating/suspending tenants and permitted staff administration; distinct from tenant staff identities.
- **Tenant Staff Account:** An independently authenticated, tenant-specific employee identity with username, registered verified email (immutable once activated), active/disabled status, and role memberships. Permanent mailbox loss results in disabling this identity and onboarding a *different* staff identity, preserving the old identity for history and audit rather than rekeying it.
- **Invitation:** One-time, tenant/recipient/initiator/initial-role onboarding invitation with a seven-day (168-hour) validity period measured from issuance, a securely enforced lifecycle state, and administrator-triggered replacement that immediately invalidates the previous link. Standalone manual cancellation is not supported in the MVP (CL-020).
- **Role:** A tenant-owned reusable collection of permission grants.
- **Permission Grant / Scope:** An allowed action within a protected area, possibly limited to All, Own, or Assigned records; domain meanings are separately defined.
- **Active Session:** A revocable authenticated device/browser session with a meaningful-activity boundary.
- **Tenant Branding:** Required organization display name and optional logo using a single shared fixed palette.
- **User Preference:** Interface locale and Dark/Light/System theme choice.
- **Audit Event:** Tamper-resistant actor, scope, action, time and safe reference to an administrative or approved mutation event.
- **Recovery Evidence:** Recorded result of protected database/file backup verification and restore rehearsal, including affected environment and date.

## Success Criteria (mandatory)

- **SC-001:** All tested cross-tenant attempts to view, mutate, reference or export protected data or private files fail for two distinct tenants; no successful cross-tenant unauthorized access.
- **SC-002:** 100% of protected tenant operations are denied when the tenant is suspended, including operations initiated by a session authenticated before suspension.
- **SC-003:** The invitation-to-activation and username/email login acceptance suite passes for two independent tenant accounts using the same real email; neither account can use the other's password. An unused invitation can activate before seven days (168 hours) from issuance but cannot activate at/after expiry or after redemption. An authorized manual reissue, only after at least 15 minutes since the last successful issuance for that pending invitation, sends a fresh seven-day invite and immediately disables all previous links; premature resend does not send or invalidate anything. A sixth resend within any rolling 24-hour window is rejected without altering the existing invitation; initial invitation issuance does not count toward the five-resend cap, and concurrent requests cannot exceed it. Unauthorized reissue, duplicate-account activation and self-service reissue are rejected. Standalone manual invite cancellation is unavailable/denied even for tenant or platform admins; invitations become unusable by expiry, redemption or superseding reissue only. Self-service username changes require only a valid authenticated session with no additional password/email verification, are tenant-unique, preserve the original account identity/permissions, and reject conflicting names. Expired-session and unauthorized cross-account/tenant changes are rejected. Arabic/non-Latin characters, numbers, whitespace, hyphens and all other characters outside `[A-Za-z_.]` are rejected during username activation and change; English letters with dots or underscores pass the complete username format only if separators occur singly between English-letter sequences; leading/trailing/consecutive separators fail. Username lengths of exactly 3 and 30 pass the length check, while lengths of 2 and 31 fail; the same checks apply during activation and subsequent changes. Case variants of the same username cannot coexist within one tenant, and each valid account can sign in regardless of input capitalization. Once activated, the registered email cannot be changed by the employee, a tenant manager, a platform Super Admin, or an API operation; attempts are denied and do not affect sign-in or recovery. Permanently lost-mailbox replacement disables old account sessions, creates a new distinct identity through an authorized seven-day email invitation, keeps the historical account/audit references intact, and does not automatically transfer work ownership.
- **SC-004:** Every defined session scenario passes: 30-day inactivity, browser restart, concurrent devices, individual revocation, password change, recovery/reset and staff disablement. For manager-assisted reset, the manager's own verified password and proper same-tenant privilege are required; the employee privately sets the new password, with no manager visibility of credentials, and reset completion revokes all old employee sessions. Both self-service and administrator-initiated password-reset links are single-use, redeemable only before 60 minutes from issuance, and rejected after redemption or at/after expiry. Issuing a newer link through either recovery path makes every older link for the same tenant staff account unusable immediately, including unexpired links; concurrent issuance and redemption tests enforce newest-link-only semantics. Across both recovery paths, a new link cannot be issued less than 300 seconds after the last issuance for that same account: tests at 299 seconds reject without invalidating the previous link; at 300 seconds issuance can proceed and invalidates all previous links. Concurrent requests cannot issue multiple links inside the cooldown, and independent tenant accounts sharing one email maintain separate cooldowns. The maximum number of issued links is 5 per rolling 24 hours for each tenant account across both reset paths: issuance 6 is denied with no side effects, at the boundary an old issuance leaves the window, and parallel requests cannot exceed the cap.
- **SC-005:** 100% of tested unauthorized role grants, self-escalation attempts and cross-tenant permission operations are refused; multi-role grants and record-scope fixtures produce the expected effective permissions. Tenant role administrators, including existing tenant managers, cannot change a role they themselves hold (permissions, record scopes, role name or description), even if no permission increase is proposed; role-edit APIs, concurrent membership changes and bypass attempts are denied, while independent authorized administrators can modify other roles within grant limits. Attempts to archive roles assigned to one or more employees (including disabled accounts) are denied without removing any assignment; archival is eligible only after explicit, authorized removal of all assignments, with an atomic zero-assignment check under concurrent operations. Archived roles remain preserved but cannot be assigned via UI/API or included among active roles; restoration is permitted to tenant managers within their grant authority, or to delegates with an explicit `Restore Role` permission and sufficient grant scope (CL-030). It is denied through UI/API for other delegates, cross-tenant targets or roles with privileges above the actor's authority, audited, and never reinstates past memberships. Permanent role deletion is unavailable in the MVP. Archived role name reuse is permitted; active role names must be unique within the tenant. A conflicting Restore Role request is rejected until an authorized rename resolves it, with no automatic replacement/merging, no inadvertent role reassignment and concurrency-safe checks (CL-031). Only authorized platform Super Admins can appoint or revoke tenant-manager authority; attempts by any tenant manager/delegate through staff invitation, promotion/demotion, custom roles or APIs must fail. Successful platform-level appointments/revocations are tenant-bound, audited and do not expose tenant-private records. A revocation of the final active manager of an active tenant is rejected; a mere pending manager invitation is not sufficient, and concurrent revocations cannot leave zero active managers. Manager demotion preserves the staff account's enabled state and all non-manager role assignments but invalidates **every pre-demotion session across devices**; protected requests and refresh attempts using those sessions fail immediately, and only a fresh login restores access under remaining permissions. No new permissions are implied if no other roles remain.
- **SC-006:** All tested major administrative events appear in the correct tenant or platform log, without secrets, and no unauthenticated or unauthorized tenant user can read/modify the log. Delegates granted staff/role audit categories cannot view, search, count, paginate, export or fetch details of settings, platform, business or other-tenant events; direct API access and stale authorization are denied. Broader explicitly authorized tenant-manager administrative audit viewing remains tenant-scoped and does not authorize access to protected business records.
- **SC-007:** Every released foundation screen is checked in both Arabic/RTL and English/LTR and Dark/Light themes; relevant keyboard-only and WCAG 2.2 AA checks pass.
- **SC-008:** Two tenants with different logos/names display the correct own organization identity; a tenant with no logo uses the neutral fallback; provider/other-tenant branding is absent.
- **SC-009:** Before Production activation, operators demonstrate a successful isolated database restore and recovery of required stored-file content from the approved automated backups.
- **SC-010:** No unapproved business module, tenant deletion, custom domain, payment/plan, MFA, color customization, dashboard or cross-tenant staff switching is present in the accepted foundation.
